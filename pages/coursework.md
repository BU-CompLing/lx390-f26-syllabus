# Coursework and Grading

In this course, you will improve one piece of writing throught the semester, while giving your classmates feedback on their chosen piece of writing. The following types of writing can be used for this course.
- A final paper for an advanced-level course in Linguistics
- A qualifying paper or dissertation chapter
- A manuscript to be submitted to a journal or peer-reviewed archival conference
- Application materials for a PhD program, postdoc position, or tenure-track position (cover letter, research statement, teaching statement, etc.)
- An oral presentation or lecture, in video format, with accompanying slide deck, handout, or poster if appropriate
- A codebase written in Python or R, with documentation, that either provides general-purpose library functions or reproduces one or more experiments or data processing pipelines described in an academic paper

## Coursework Descriptions

You will complete the following coursework items throughout the semester. The paper presentation and the two peer review rounds are graded on completeness. The final paper is graded on quality of writing.

### Paper Presentation

During Weeks 3–4, you will present your paper to the class. Your presentation may be in any format you like, and students will have the opportunity to ask questions and discuss. The goal of this presentation is to familiarize the class with the subject matter of your paper, so that your classmates will be able to give you feedback on your writing. You may edit your paper before each peer review round.

### Peer Review 1 (Conceptual)

You will review two of your classmates' papers, focusing on the title, abstract, and introduction, or equivalent parts of other writing types. Your review will evaluate whether the paper has a good motivation, thesis statement, and argument supporting that statement, and whether these elements are effectively communicated. Effective academic papers need to "solve a puzzle" within the target audience's paradigm [(Kuhn, 1962, Chapter 4)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Structure_of_Scientific_Revolutions).

### Peer Review 2 (Mechanical)

You will review two more of your classmates' papers. This time, you will assume that each paper has a sound motivation, thesis statement, and argument, and your focus will be on whether this is communicated effectively. Effective writers distribute information uniformly throughout the paper [(Jaeger and Levy, 2006)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2006/hash/c6a01432c8138d46ba39957a8250e027-Abstract.html), and they do this by signposting new information while minimizing semantic redundancy.

### Final Paper

At the end of the semester, you will submit a revised version of your paper, which will be graded on the quality of its writing. You are encouraged, but not required, to incorporate whatever feedback from your peer reviewers you deem appropriate. If your chosen piece of writing is a presentation, you may present it to the class instead of submitting a video of your presentation.

## Grading 

You will receive a final _raw grade_ out of 100 points, calculated as follows.

| Item                       | Released | Due   | Points  |

| Participation              |          |       | 10      |
| Paper Presentation         |          |       | 20      |
| Peer Review 1 (Conceptual) | 10/12    | 10/26 | 20      |
| Peer Review 2 (Mechanical) | 11/9     | 11/23 | 20      |

Full credit for participation is granted automatically, except in exceptional cases as determined by Sophie.

Release dates and due dates shown above are tentative and may be subject to change.

## Submissions and Deadlines

Unless stated otherwise, all submission deadlines are on **Mondays at 9:00 AM**. The final paper is due on **Friday, December 18, at 11:59 PM**.

#### Late Submissions

Unless stated otherwise, peer reviews that are submitted late will be penalized by 0.2 points (1 percentage point) for each hour, full or partial, that they are late. The minimum possible grade for a homework assignment is 0 points.

For example, a fully complete peer review will receive a grade of…

- 20 points if it is submitted at or before 9:00 AM on the due date
- 19.8 points (99%) if it is submitted at 9:30 AM on the due date
- 15.2 points (76%) if it is submitted at 9:00 AM on the day after the due date
- 0 points if it is submitted at or after 1:01 PM four days after the due date.

#### Grace Period and Extensions

You will have a grace period of 168 hours (7 days) over the entire semester during which you can submit late work without penalty. You do not have to ask permission to use the grace period; it will be calculated at the end of the semester so as to optimize your final grade.

Extensions for documented emergencies may be granted at Sophie's discretion. Extensions will only be granted when there is a legitimate reason not to use your 168-hour grace period.

In some cases, extensions may be granted to the entire class without a late penalty.

## Letter Grades

Your letter grade will be calculated according to the following scale, where points are rounded to the nearest .01.

| Raw Grade | Letter Grade |
| --------- | ------------ |
| 93–1000   | A            |
| 90–92.99  | A-           |
| 88–89.99  | B+           |
| 83–87.99  | B            |
| 0–82.99   | B-           |

Grades of C+, F, or I may be assigned in exceptional cases as determined by Sophie, such as failure to submit a final exam. Depending on the distribution of raw grades in the class, you may receive a higher letter grade than what is indicated above. You will never receive a letter grade lower than the grade indicated above, unless you are assigned a grade of C+, F, or I. You will never receive a lower letter grade than another student whose raw grade is equal to or lower than yours, unless either you or the other student has been assigned a grade of C+, F, or I.
